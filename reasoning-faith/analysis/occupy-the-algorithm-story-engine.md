# The Story-Driving Engine of *Occupy the Algorithm*

**A structural analysis for the author of *Reasoning Faith***

*Source: manuscript of Evan DiDio's* Occupy the Algorithm *(working title* YouTube for Churches*), read in full from the author's working Google Doc. The document contains the complete ten-chapter draft plus two later editorial layers (an April improvement chart and a January partial revision). This analysis works from the main draft, citing the revision layers only where they confirm a structural point. Web searches surfaced no substantial indexed publication data on the released edition, so all observations are anchored to the manuscript text. Nothing in the file appeared truncated.*

---

## Why this book is worth your study

*Occupy the Algorithm* is not a well-polished book. The draft carries placeholder screenshots, an orphaned outline line ("Illustration: The funnel and container analogy"), and visible repetition between chapters. And yet it **moves**. A reader who opens Chapter 1 is still reading at Chapter 6, and the reason is almost entirely structural, not stylistic. DiDio has an instinctive story-driving engine under his teaching, and because the craftsmanship around it is rough, the engine is unusually easy to see. That makes it a better teaching specimen for you than a seamless book would be: you can watch the machine run, and you can watch exactly where it dies.

One framing note before the details. DiDio's surface register — jokes, internet slang, exclamation points — is not what makes the book move, and none of it transfers to *Reasoning Faith*. What transfers is underneath the jokes: **a person who is present on the page, a mission that is always at stake, and a reader who is repeatedly addressed, examined, and charged.** Every recommendation in the closing playbook is built from those three elements, not from the comedy.

## 1. Chapter openings: the promise before the teaching

DiDio opens ten chapters six different ways, and the pattern is instructive: the strong openings all put a **person or a failure** on the page before any instruction appears, and each one makes an implicit promise the chapter then keeps.

| Ch. | Opening move | First beat | Promise made to the reader |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cold open, personal scene** | "I'll never forget that Wednesday night service when a witch joined our stream." | The camera reaches the lost; this book shows you what that camera can do. |
| 2 | **Blunt direct address** | "Let me be blunt—if your titles and thumbnails aren't driving value, they're driving people away." | Your message is dying in its packaging; I will fix the packaging. |
| 3 | **The reader's own excuse, voiced** | "I hear it all the time: 'We just don't have the time or resources...'" | Your excuse is real, and it is about to be dismantled. |
| 4 | **Named mistake / warning** | A "tragic mistake" that is "literally KILLING their YouTube channel." | I will name the mistake you are making right now, then repair it. |
| 5 | **Contrast scene** | The warm in-person greeter at the door — set against silence when someone joins the stream. | You are unknowingly neglecting real people; I will show you how to welcome them. |
| 6 | **Consulting-room story** | "I once sat down with a mega-ministry that was struggling to gain traction..." | The data will show you what you cannot see about yourself. |
| 7 | **Contrarian thesis** | Adsense — the thing everyone assumes is the answer — declared his "least favorite" funding method. | The obvious answer is wrong; better ones follow. |
| 8 | **Concept statement** (no story) | YouTube defined as a search engine. | None beyond "here is information." |
| 9 | **Concept statement**, example arrives late | Keyword research declared important; the "Prophetic Word" example enters in paragraph three. | Weak; largely repeats Chapter 8's promise. |
| 10 | **Testimony of a working model** | "This isn't just theory; it's a real-world example of how we've built..." | What follows has been lived, not theorized. |

Three observations for your book:

**The cold opens are the best pages in the manuscript.** The witch anecdote (Ch. 1) and the mega-ministry consult (Ch. 6) drop the reader into a moment already in progress — no preamble, no "in this chapter we will." Both are also the chapter's doctrine in miniature: the witch *is* "the camera represents the lost masses" before that thesis is ever stated; the countdown-loving ministry *is* "you cannot see yourself without data" before analytics are defined.

**The voiced-objection open (Ch. 3) is the most transferable move for an apologetics book.** DiDio quotes the reader's excuse verbatim, concedes its force ("And I get it"), then turns: "But here's the reality." This is the classic objection–concession–answer arc, and it creates instant intimacy: the reader feels caught. For a self-convicting book, opening a chapter with the reader's own unspoken objection — stated fairly, in the reader's voice — is the single strongest way to establish that the author knows whom he is talking to.

**Chapters 8 and 9 open without a promise, and they are the weakest chapters in the book.** More on this in Section 5, but note the correlation now: opening quality predicts chapter momentum almost perfectly across all ten chapters.

## 2. The anecdote–instruction weave, mapped

Here is where story enters, where teaching takes over, and where story returns, in the four most instructive chapters.

### Chapter 1 — The full sandwich, plus a stakes close

1. **Story (cold open):** the witch in the livestream — a lost person, drawn in, "a seed that someone else will water."
2. **Thesis, framed as the reader's error:** the "#1 mistake" of assuming online viewers are just local congregants.
3. **Evidence burst:** three statistics (33% check a channel before attending; 50–195% attendance growth; tithing up 70%).
4. **Second story, historical:** Dr. Lester Sumrall buying TV stations while other pastors preached against television — then the accountability scene: DiDio imagining explaining to Sumrall, in Heaven, that he had the same power in his smartphone and never used it.
5. **Theological argument:** Paul's Roman roads; "Today, we use digital highways"; the unchanged commission.
6. **Direct challenge to the reader:** "...a generation ripe for harvest - what do you see?"
7. **Objection voiced and demolished:** "But our church is small, can we really reach millions?"
8. **Worked example (teaching proper begins):** the sermon title "7 Steps to the Promised Land" dismantled problem by problem.
9. **Confession:** "To be honest I'm not the best marketer."
10. **Stakes close:** people "saved, delivered, set free" because the team "spent 15 minutes on a thumbnail/title" — then the charge: "so let's get to work."

Notice the ratio: teaching does not fully take over until beat 8, more than halfway through the chapter — and by then the reader has met a witch, a dead evangelist, the Apostle Paul, and his own excuse. The instruction lands on soil that has been plowed.

### Chapter 2 — The inverted sandwich: story held back for the close

Chapter 2 opens on argument, not anecdote — but it works because the argument is aimed at a **concrete villain**: the video titled "Sunday Morning 9-8-2024." DiDio spends real emotion on it ("Nothing frustrates me more..."), translates the sin into consequence ("It's like writing 'DO NOT WATCH' on the thumbnail"), then teaches: title principles, "Evan's thumbnail laws," and a seven-step viewer journey. Then — and this is the craft move — the story arrives **last**:

- A real video is named: "3 Things Kim Clement Repented of before he died."
- Suspense hinge: "This video FLOPPED in the first couple of days. Let me explain…"
- Failure quantified (about 1,000 views in 24 hours), diagnosis, intervention (new title and thumbnail), result (200,000 views in days; past 460,000 total).
- Conviction line as the takeaway: "I choose to believe the performance is my fault if it underperforms."

The case study proves everything the chapter just taught, retroactively converting a list of tips into a tested method. A chapter that ends on its best story feels finished; a chapter that ends on its last list feels abandoned. Compare this with Chapter 9's closing "Real-World Example," which occupies the same structural slot but contains no scene, no timeline, and no new numbers — and evaporates on contact.

### Chapter 4 — Micro-drama inside the mechanism

Chapter 4 must explain algorithm mechanics — the driest material in the book — and it stays alive through three devices:

1. **Physical analogy up front:** "Rerouting water into two streams reduces the flow of each." One sentence, and the whole chapter's logic is portable.
2. **The algorithm as a character.** DiDio narrates YouTube's inner monologue — the platform "thinking" that a rebroadcast "is not what they subscribed for." Mechanism becomes micro-drama; the reader follows a *decision*, not a specification.
3. **The one-line payoff.** His church stops streaming to Facebook and Vimeo; he asks, "Can you guess what happened?" — then a single-sentence paragraph: "We doubled in viewers instantly." The white space does the shouting.

Then the confession beat: churches believe they must stream to Facebook "for the older congregants" — "I know this is a lie because we believed it too." He taught his older members YouTube one Wednesday night and found most already used it; the punchline is delivered as a subhead: 28.2% of 2024 views came from viewers 65 and older. Story, self-implication, and data arrive as one unit.

### Chapter 6 — Story as diagnosis, and the only dialogue in the book

The mega-ministry open is the manuscript's most sophisticated weave because the story *performs* the chapter's thesis. The ministry defends its 90-second countdown — "We actually like our 90-second countdown. People think that's fun" — the only quoted dialogue in the book, and it is spent exactly where self-deception needs a voice. Then the analytics deliver the verdict: only 3% watched the countdown; more than 60% of viewers were lost during it. The numbers convict where argument could not — which is precisely the chapter's claim about analytics.

Teaching then takes over for a long stretch (metric definitions, new-vs-returning viewers, channel organization), and the pulse is kept by two devices: a pastoral reassurance placed mid-density ("These numbers are not to intimidate you — they're tools to help you serve better") and a second-person audit list ("Are you posting consistently, or has it been a few months?"). Story returns at the close — the Encounter Today case study — and the chapter ends on a sharp personality coda, "The 'CCLI' Lie," warning that licensing worship music doesn't fix YouTube copyright: "someone else is now making money from you." Sandwich complete: scene → teaching → case → barb.

**The pattern across all four:** story opens (or a villain does), teaching occupies the middle with its pulse maintained by second-person devices, and story or consequence returns to close. Where any of the three layers is missing — Chapters 8, 9, and the middle of 10 — momentum dies measurably.

## 3. Momentum devices

**Questions aimed at the reader.** Not rhetorical wallpaper — questions that demand a private answer: "what do you see?" (Ch. 1, closing the harvest image); "Have you ever searched for that?" (Ch. 1, deflating the sermon title); "Can you guess what happened?" (Ch. 4, before the payoff); and the Chapter 6 channel-audit battery, five consecutive second-person questions about the reader's own channel. The audit battery is the most important for your purposes: it converts a teaching section into an examination the reader performs on himself.

**Imperative micro-commands.** "Read that again" (after the sermon-title claim), "Let's fix that," "let's get to work." Two or three words that grab the reader's collar between paragraphs.

**The "Let me explain…" hinge.** Used twice at load-bearing moments (the Kim Clement flop, Ch. 2; the buying-views case, Ch. 3): a surprising claim is made, then explanation is briefly *withheld*. A one-line debt the next paragraph pays.

**Headings that argue instead of label.** DiDio's subheads are frequently full declarative sentences — "What a shame it would be if we didn't evangelize the world at a time when it is easiest to do so"; "If loving people is your priority, then social media is your priority..."; "Consolidation is key"; "Many visitors will watch you online before they ever come in person"; even a data point promoted to a subhead (the 28.2% line). A reader skimming only the headings of Chapters 1–6 receives the entire argument under pressure. Where the headings revert to labels ("Understanding Keywords and Keyword Phrases," "Filling Out Descriptions, Tags, and More"), the chapters stall.

**One-line paragraphs as beats.** "We doubled in viewers instantly." The manuscript's best rhythm device: density, then a single short line carrying the payload.

**Named, owned frameworks.** "Evan's thumbnail laws." The ASQ (Answer Specific Questions) strategy. "Chop the Chicken." "The 'CCLI' Lie." Naming does two jobs: it stamps the author's personality onto generic material, and it makes the idea *recallable* — Chapter 6 can say "remember the ASQ method" and retrieve a whole teaching in four words instead of repeating it. (Note the ownership language transfers without the whimsy: "laws," "the lie," "the strategy.")

**Lists that serve momentum vs. lists that stall it.** The dividing line in this manuscript is unmistakable: **a list moves when it is secretly a narrative or an examination, and stalls when it is inventory.**

- *Moving:* the 5-day, 30-minutes-a-day plan (Ch. 3) — each item is a day, so the list is a week the reader lives through; the 7-step viewer journey (Ch. 2) — eyes → expression → text → title → timestamp → description → first seconds — a story of one viewer's ten seconds, told as a list; the Chapter 6 audit questions — every item is second person and convicting.
- *Stalling:* the tool inventory in Chapter 7 (Shopify, Teespring, Kajabi, Mailchimp...); the eight-role organizational chart occupying the heart of Chapter 10 (roughly a hundred lines of role/responsibility pairs — reference matter parked in the chapter body); the metric-definition blocks that appear in Chapter 6 and again, re-explained, in Chapters 8 and 9.

**FAQ blocks that voice objections.** Chapter 2 pauses to answer "What if it feels like clickbait?" and "Can I just reuse my sermon title?" — the reader's resistance is named and cleared before it can accumulate. This is momentum maintenance: an unanswered objection is drag on every subsequent page.

**Closing charges.** The strong chapters end with an act, not a summary: "I want to encourage you to experiment by updating your past videos" (Ch. 3); "Actionable Step: Start by identifying 3-5 keyword phrases..." (Ch. 9 — the one strong beat in that chapter). The reader leaves the chapter with homework, which is another name for a reason to come back.

## 4. Stakes: how a how-to book keeps a mission burning

This is the book's deepest lesson for *Reasoning Faith*, because DiDio faces your exact problem in mirror image: he must keep a reader emotionally engaged through *technical* instruction, as you must through *doctrinal* instruction. His solution has five components.

**1. The eternal welded to the mundane — in single sentences.** The signature move of the whole book: "We've seen people saved, delivered, set free from addictions, healed, and eternally changed because we spent 15 minutes on a thumbnail/title." One sentence fuses the largest possible outcome (eternity) to the smallest possible act (fifteen minutes on a graphic). He does it again with the camera "sitting in the back of your sanctuary" that "represents the lost masses" — an object every church reader can see from their pew, reframed as a mission field. The stakes are never a separate devotional section; they are *soldered onto the instruction itself*, so the reader cannot receive the how-to without the why.

**2. Accountability before God, made concrete as a scene.** The Sumrall passage does not say "we will answer for our stewardship." It stages the answering: DiDio sitting across from Sumrall in Heaven, admitting he carried in his pocket what Sumrall spent millions on, and never used it. The humor around the scene ("Irish right-hook") is removable; the structure — *imagine giving the account, to a specific person, of the specific resource you wasted* — is a fully self-convicting device that needs no comedy at all. It is Nathan's "thou art the man," built from church-media budgets.

**3. Neglect converted into a moral image.** A generic title is "writing 'DO NOT WATCH' on the thumbnail." An unmanned live chat is a visitor entering the sanctuary and being met by no one — "Just as you wouldn't ignore someone who walks into your church, you shouldn't ignore someone who joins your online stream." Technical negligence is consistently re-described as a failure of love toward a particular, imaginable person. The reader is never allowed to file a mistake under "suboptimal"; it is filed under "someone was turned away."

**4. Urgency of the hour, stated as shame at a missed opportunity.** "What a shame it would be if we didn't evangelize the world at a time when it is easiest to do so" — elevated to a subhead so the skimmer cannot miss it. Paired with the enemy's counter-strategy (distraction by "those immediate fires we have to put out"), the cost of waiting is framed as both historical failure and spiritual warfare.

**5. Stakes renewed at density points.** The stakes are not front-loaded and abandoned. Mid-way through the analytics chapter: the numbers exist "to help you serve better." Closing the monetization chapter: the income exists "to advance the Kingdom of God." Whenever the material threatens to become merely technical, one sentence re-tethers it to the mission — a discipline worth stealing wholesale: *every chapter's densest section gets one mission-sentence, placed inside it, not before or after it.*

## 5. Where the book stalls — and what the stalls teach

**Chapter 8 (SEO) is the control group.** No opening story, no named person, no case study; it defines terms and enumerates fields to fill out. Personality resurfaces exactly once — the "Tags are Dead?" aside, which admits the earlier tags advice is largely obsolete and lands the chapter's only real insight: "what works today may not work tomorrow." That aside proves the author was available the whole time; the structure simply never called him onto the page. Chapter 8 is what every chapter would read like without the engine.

**Chapter 9's case-study slot is filled with abstraction.** The chapter leans on a single example — "Prophetic Word" — recycled at least five times across its sections, and its closing "Real-World Example" contains no scene, no dates, no before/after, and no numbers beyond the search volume already cited twice. Set beside Chapter 2's Kim Clement study (named video, quantified flop, intervention, 200,000-view reversal), the lesson is stark: **a case study is a story with numbers, and a story requires a specific event.** A generalized success summary occupying a story's structural position is worse than no story — it teaches the reader that the story slots can be skimmed.

**Chapters 8 and 9 also demonstrate repetition-as-stall.** Both chapters teach keywords; Chapters 4 and 6 both teach new-vs-returning viewers; "value-driven" is re-argued in at least four chapters. Some repetition is deliberate reinforcement, but here it is *unmarked* — the book re-explains rather than recalls, and the reader feels the wheels spin. The embedded editorial audit flags this too. The contrast is DiDio's own best practice elsewhere: ASQ is taught once, named, and afterward merely *invoked* ("remember the ASQ method"). Named once, recalled thereafter, with something new added at each return — that is repetition as architecture. Re-explained anonymously — that is repetition as stall. Given that repetition is a known weakness of *Reasoning Faith*, this contrast may be the single most valuable diagnostic in the source book.

**Chapter 10's middle is a filing cabinet.** The chapter opens and closes warmly — and its close contains a genuinely self-convicting beat, DiDio admitting mid-sentence that "even at the time of writing this book" he feels he must appreciate his volunteers more; the author examining himself on the page in real time. But the middle is an eight-role organizational chart, pure reference matter, and the reader vaults over it. The fix the manuscript itself gestures toward (it has a "Reference Section" stub) is quarantine: chapter bodies for argument and story, appendices for inventories.

**Chapter 5 shows a subtler failure: a scene promised, then never peopled.** The greeter-versus-silence opening is vivid, but the chapter never introduces one actual online member — no name, no message from the chat, no follow-up story. The witch of Chapter 1 remains the book's only individual online viewer. The teaching on "treating online viewers like real people" would be doubled in force by a single real person; the book knows this and doesn't do it. (The later audit layer asks for exactly such a testimonial — confirmation that the gap was felt.)

**The unifying diagnostic:** everywhere the book moves, a person is on the page — the author, a named ministry, a voiced objector, an anthropomorphized algorithm. Everywhere it stalls, the person has left, and the prose could be transplanted into any manual on the subject without a seam. Personality, in this genre, is not tone. It is *presence* — someone specific speaking to someone specific about something that happened.

## 6. The playbook: twelve transferable moves for *Reasoning Faith*

Extracted for a KJV, formal-register apologetics/discipleship book that must remain educational and self-convicting, with no imported humor. Each move names its source in *Occupy the Algorithm* and its translation into your book's terms.

1. **Open every chapter inside a witnessed moment, and make the moment the doctrine in miniature.** (The witch; the mega-ministry consult.) A conversation with a doubter, a question asked after a lesson, an hour of the author's own wrestling with a text — begun mid-scene, no preamble. The scene must *contain* the chapter's thesis before the thesis is stated, so the teaching arrives as the explanation of something the reader has already watched happen.

2. **Make the chapter's promise explicit on its first page.** Every strong DiDio opening tells the reader what failing or question the chapter resolves ("tragic mistake... KILLING their channel"; "We just don't have the time"). In your register: name the doubt, the error, or the neglected duty this chapter answers — as a plain declarative sentence — before the exposition begins. A chapter that opens without a promise reads as reference, and reference is skimmed.

3. **Voice the reader's objection in the reader's own words, concede its force, then answer it.** (Chapter 3's excuse; Chapter 1's "But our church is small"; the FAQ blocks.) This is apologetics' native motion turned inward: steelman the objection the reader has *privately* held — about prayer, about evidence, about his own lukewarmness — state it more honestly than he would dare, then reason it down from Scripture. Self-conviction begins the moment the reader hears his secret thought read aloud.

4. **Build the sandwich: never let a chapter end on pure instruction.** (Kim Clement closing Chapter 2; "The CCLI Lie" closing Chapter 6.) End on a returned story, a consequence, or a charge. And fill every case-study slot with an actual event — specific persons, a before, an intervention, an after — never a generalized success summary (the Chapter 9 failure). In your book the "numbers" of a case study are the particulars: the verse, the day, the man, what he said, what changed.

5. **Write headings that argue.** ("What a shame it would be..."; "Consolidation is key.") Convert topic labels into declarative convictions so that the skimming reader receives the chapter's argument under pressure from the headings alone. For a book built on Scripture, a heading can carry the weight of a thesis or a text — it should never carry merely a category.

6. **Deploy the one-line paragraph as a conviction beat.** ("We doubled in viewers instantly." / "Read that again.") After every dense doctrinal passage, land one short sentence in its own paragraph that carries the consequence. This is also the repair for essay-drone: continuous essay has no beats; a beat every few pages gives the reader somewhere to stand.

7. **Interrogate the reader at intervals — the audit battery.** (Chapter 6's five consecutive second-person questions about the reader's own channel.) DiDio audits your channel; *Reasoning Faith* can audit the soul, which is precisely its stated aim. Convert one teaching section per chapter into direct questions the reader must answer about himself, anchored in the chapter's text — the "Examine yourselves" (2 Cor. 13:5) pattern given structural form. This is the mechanism by which a book *becomes* self-convicting rather than merely describing conviction.

8. **Weld eternal stakes to the specific act being taught — in single sentences, placed inside the dense sections.** ("...eternally changed because we spent 15 minutes on a thumbnail.") Not a devotional paragraph appended to a chapter, but one sentence fused to the instruction: what the unexamined argument costs, whom the unprepared answer fails, what hangs on the discipline being taught. And renew the stakes at the density points, as DiDio does mid-analytics — one mission-sentence *inside* every technical stretch.

9. **Confess before you charge.** ("I know this is a lie because we believed it too"; "To be honest I'm not the best marketer"; the Chapter 10 admission that he must do better by his volunteers, made in real time on the page.) The author's self-implication is what licenses the book's demands — conviction lands because the author visibly goes first. This is DiDio's warmth with the humor removed, and it is the direct remedy for a book that struggles with personal connection: the fastest route to the reader's heart is through the author's own examined one.

10. **Name your frameworks once; thereafter recall, never re-explain — and add one new turn at each return.** (ASQ taught once, then invoked by name; versus keywords re-explained anonymously across Chapters 8–9.) For a book with a repetition problem this is the central discipline: give each recurring principle a fixed, sober name at first teaching, then let later chapters summon it in a phrase and *advance* it. Repetition with a name and an escalation is architecture; repetition without them is drone.

11. **Quarantine reference matter.** (Chapter 10's org chart; Chapter 7's tool inventory — the pages readers vault over.) Definitions, catalogued proofs, lists of sources, and any material meant for looking-up belong in appendices or clearly-marked reference sections. The chapter body is reserved for argument, story, and examination. A list may remain in-chapter only if it is secretly a narrative (the 5-day plan) or an examination (the audit questions) — that is, only if it moves.

12. **End every chapter with a charge, not a summary.** ("...experiment by updating your past videos"; "identify 3-5 keyword phrases... and implement [them] in your next video.") Close with an act of obedience or examination the reader performs before opening the next chapter — a passage to read on his knees, an answer to write, a conversation to have. A summary discharges the chapter's tension; a charge converts it into motion, and motion is what carries a reader through a teaching book.

---

### The one-sentence version

*Occupy the Algorithm* moves because a specific man with an eternal mission keeps stepping onto the page — telling what he saw, confessing what he got wrong, and asking the reader questions the reader must answer alone; it stalls the moment he steps off. Keep the man on the page of *Reasoning Faith* — reverent, examined, and direct — and the essay problem solves itself.
