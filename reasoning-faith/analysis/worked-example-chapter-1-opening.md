# Worked Example — Rebuilding the Chapter 1 Opening

This document applies the whole packet to one concrete case: the opening of
Chapter 1, "The Mind Under Siege." It shows the anatomy of the current
opening, the rebuilt architecture, and model prose for each new beat. Model
passages marked **[VIGNETTE]** are *shapes with slots*: the true details
belong to the author and must replace the bracketed placeholders — nothing
in a vignette may be invented on his behalf (see `notes/story-inventory.md`,
rule 1).

---

## 1. Anatomy of the current opening

The chapter opens with a single ~450-word paragraph that: announces a war,
catalogs how beliefs enter (four appositive clauses before the first period
ends the ear's patience), states the thesis ("every mind is being
discipled"), pivots to the lectern ("I want to name the war more precisely"),
runs a three-rung Before-ladder, and closes by cross-examining the reader.

Diagnoses from the audits, all visible in this one paragraph:

- **Lecture posture.** The war is announced from a podium; no person, no
  room, no time of day. The reader is watched, not accompanied.
- **The accidental confession.** Mid-paragraph the sermon's "you" slips into
  "my" ("…then *wonder* why my faith feels unstable"). Grammatically an
  error; editorially a signal. The confession keeps trying to leak through
  the lecture.
- **Shape repetition.** The Before-ladder ("Before faith is abandoned…
  Before a generation…") plus "not X, and it is not Y. It is Z" plus the
  interrogative battery, all in the first page — three of the six catalogued
  skeletons before the reader reaches paragraph two.
- **Run-ons and splices** where clauses accumulate without a full stop.

## 2. The rebuilt architecture

Per the eight-beat template (`MASTER-PLAN.md` §3):

| Beat | Content |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Cold open | **[VIGNETTE A]** — the night the author noticed a thought in his head that he had never chosen (below) |
| 2. Turn / loop | "Where did that sentence come from?" — paused, unanswered |
| 3. Ascend | *Then* the war paragraph, now landing as testimony |
| 4. Argument | Gate, *natsar*, walls; ONE word study; the atheism material trimmed to a pointer (its home is Ch. 2) |
| 5. Vulnerability beat | **[VIGNETTE B]** — the missed chance to be someone's light (the broken-person paragraph, disguise off) |
| 6. Close the loop | Return to the night from beat 1: name where that sentence had come from; final line leans into Ch. 2's question |
| 7–8. Reflection + prayer | The discernment checklist survives (it is a tool); prayer echoes the gate and the night |

## 3. Model prose

### Beat 1 — the cold open [VIGNETTE A]

The spec: a particular night, a particular room, one intrusive borrowed
thought rendered verbatim, the horror that it sounded like the author's own
voice. Model shape (placeholders in brackets):

> There is a sentence I can still hear in my own voice, though I know now
> that the voice was borrowed. It arrived on [an ordinary night, placed and
> dated], while I was [the true mundane activity], and it said, plainly,
> [the borrowed thought, quoted]. I did not argue with it. That is the part
> that troubles me all these years later. I had argued with weaker ideas for
> sport. This one I welcomed like a familiar guest, because by then it had
> knocked so many times that opening the door no longer felt like a
> decision.

Then the turn (beat 2):

> I have since asked myself a question I would ask you to hold, unanswered,
> for the length of this chapter. Who wrote that sentence? I know I did not.
> I never sat down and reasoned my way to it. It was simply there one night,
> wearing my voice, speaking with my authority. Something had been handed to
> me, and I had signed for it without reading it.

### Beat 3 — the ascent (the war paragraph, resequenced)

The existing argument survives nearly whole; it simply lands differently
because a witness is speaking. The Before-ladder is replaced with a moving
sequence, and the slip is resolved by *committing* to the confession rather
than correcting it back to "your":

> There is a war most people do not realize they are fighting, and I fought
> it for years without knowing its name. It does not begin with armies or
> headlines. It begins with a thought repeated until it feels
> unquestionable, or a wound that becomes an explanation for God. Long
> before a belief is defended in public, it has been formed in private,
> because every mind is being discipled. Mine was. The war is not fought
> over your intelligence or your sincerity. It is fought over your access.
> First a person stops guarding the door, then truth is renegotiated in the
> dark, and only long after does the mouth announce what the heart settled
> quietly. I let every philosophy, trend, and voice that arrived often
> enough disciple my mind, and then wondered why my faith felt unstable.

(Note: one "It is fought over your access" contrastive definition is kept —
that is the chapter's ration. The atheism-burden-of-proof paragraph moves to
Chapter 2 with a one-sentence pointer, per the idea-homes table.)

### Beat 5 — the vulnerability beat [VIGNETTE B]

The current draft circles this story in the third person ("we almost never
see who has been broken over and over… because of selfishness or
inconvenience"). The rebuilt beat takes the disguise off the author while
veiling the other person (ethics rules 2 and 7):

> I am not writing this as a man who guarded his gate well. There was
> [a person, unidentifiable] whom I saw [the true frequency and setting],
> and I knew, the way you know weather, that something in them was breaking.
> I chose convenience [the true specific choice]. I have prayed since then
> to be interruptible. The gate I left unmanned that season was not only the
> one facing inward. An unguarded mind does not merely let the wrong things
> in. It stops noticing what it was posted to protect.

### Beat 6 — closing the loop

> Which brings me back to the sentence that was waiting for me that night.
> I traced it eventually. It had come from [the true source, named
> generically: a feed, a friendship, a professor, a grief], repeated until
> it sounded like me. The tracing took years. The letting in had taken no
> time at all. That asymmetry is the whole war, and it raises the question
> the next chapter must answer: if even my own thoughts can arrive
> secondhand, what would it mean to test them? What is faith made of, if
> not borrowed certainty?

## 4. Line-level repairs (independent of the vignettes)

1. **The slip:** "…then *wonder* why my faith feels unstable" — resolve by
   owning it (as above), not by reverting to "your."
2. **The opening sentence:** four appositive clauses before the first
   period. Split at the strongest joint; the clauses become a moving
   sequence across two sentences.
3. **Comma splices:** "Everything truly valuable is guarded, it is
   protected…" → subordinate or split.
4. **The interrogative battery** ("Who and what has been walking… Not who
   you argue with but what is really feeding you? Entertainment? pleasure?…")
   — replaced by the single held question from beat 2, re-asked once, after
   the confession, where it now reads as companionship: "So I ask you what I
   was finally forced to ask myself. Who has been standing at your gate?"
5. **Word-study budget:** keep *natsar* (it serves the chapter's central
   image); the *access* dictionary sidebar stays (fresh device, used once);
   Colossians 2:8's "spoil" study moves to wherever the philosophy section
   settles, so the chapter carries one study.

## 5. Why this works (the packet, compressed)

The rebuilt opening runs the story engine (a promise, a held question, a
payoff that means more at the close), puts the author on the page before the
reader is examined (confession before charge), keeps the book's formal,
self-convicting register (no humor imported; the conviction is derived and
sequenced, not softened), rations the skeletons (one ladder, one contrastive
definition, one held question), and turns the chapter's thesis from an
assertion into an exhibit. The reader does not have to believe that every
mind is being discipled. They have just watched it happen to the author.
