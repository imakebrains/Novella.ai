# Reasoning Faith — Repetition & Structure Audit

**Prepared for:** Drew Peckham
**Documents reviewed:**
1. *Reasoning Faith* — current master (Google Doc). Chapters 1–3 drafted; Chapters 4–6 are empty placeholders. Read in full; no truncation.
2. *Reasoning Faith — MASTER V2* (Google Doc). Complete 15-chapter manuscript, ~96,000 words including front matter, Introduction, and per-chapter end matter. Read in full (586,776 characters retrieved); the file ends naturally with Chapter 15's closing prayer — **no truncation** in what I received.

**The complaint being audited:** the prose "feels super repetitive without being repetitive." That instinct is exactly right, and this report names why. Almost no phrases repeat verbatim. What repeats is *shape*: roughly six sentence skeletons, one chapter template, one argumentative move, and five ideas — cycled with fresh vocabulary for 96,000 words. The ear counts shapes, not words. Once a reader's ear has learned the shapes (and it learns them by Chapter 3), every subsequent chapter is a song they already know.

One piece of good news up front: the current master's rewrites of Chapters 1–3 already fix the worst of V2's habits. The direction is right. But the rewrite is importing a few new tics of its own, flagged below, and the deeper structural repetition can't be fixed at the sentence level at all.

## 1. Sentence-Shape Repetition: The Skeleton Catalog

These are the recurring skeletons, ordered by how much of the "same-shape drone" each one causes. Counts are approximate (pattern-matched across the full V2 text, hand-verified in samples) and are meant to show scale, not forensic precision.

### 1a. The Not-ladder — the book's signature tic

*Shape:* a stack of one-line negations ending in a one-word landing.

> "Not matter. Not chaos. Not nothing. Not chance. God." (Ch. 4)

This is a genuinely strong move — the Chapter 4 instance above is the best sentence-cluster in the book. The problem is it recurs everywhere with swapped nouns, so the reader hears the *same drumbeat* in chapter after chapter:

- Ch. 5: evil is "Not painful. Not unpleasant. Not socially undesirable. Evil."
- Ch. 6: the identical move one chapter later — not inconvenient, not unpleasant, not against my preference: *Wrong.* (Chapters 5 and 6 open with the *same* ladder making the *same* point.)
- Ch. 13: the witness is not someone perfect, not polished, not performing — *but changed* (then seven "Someone who should be X, but Y" lines).
- Ch. 14: five consecutive "Not merely…" lines before "Who is Jesus?"
- Ch. 15: "Not in theory. Not in religious language…" followed by a **fourteen-item** "In you. In your obedience. In your…" ladder.
- Even the **Dedication** opens the book with a five-rung "I hated…" anaphora — the reader meets the ladder before they meet page one.

Sentence-initial "Not…" fragments alone number **~115 across V2**, clustering heaviest in Ch. 8 (18), Ch. 9 (21), and Ch. 15 (20). In the current master, the count is lower but present (11 across three chapters).

### 1b. The contrastive definition: "X is not Y. It is Z."

*Shape:* define by negation, then land the true definition as its own short sentence. Examples (paraphrased): faith is not a feeling — it is a title deed; a stronghold is not a bad mood — it is a fortified place; an idol is not only a statue — it is anything trusted above God; truth is not an abstraction — it is a Person. In the current master's Chapter 2 alone this shape appears **seven times**; across the three rewritten chapters, about **thirteen times in 11,300 words** — once every 850 words, which is often enough for the ear to flag it as a formula. Add the close cousins ("does not X. It Y…" ~90 instances in V2; "will not X. It will Y") and this is the most frequent single skeleton in the book.

### 1c. The anaphora catalog

*Shape:* three-to-ten sentences launched from the same stem with rotated objects.

- Ch. 8: "Some people live without church. Some live without prayer. Some live without Scripture…" then "You may trust reason. You may trust science. You may trust your feelings…" (a **seventeen-item** trust catalog in one paragraph).
- Ch. 9: "Some bow to money. Some bow to certainty. Some bow to approval…" — the identical skeleton one chapter later, nouns swapped. Then: "It says, 'I am just being careful.' It says, 'I am just being responsible.'…" (five rungs).
- Ch. 6: "We do not first consult… We do not need a committee… We do not need a survey…"
- Ch. 12: "A sunset breaks… A song rises… A child laughs… A mountain stands…"
- Intro: "Some reject Him because… Some reject Him because…" (×4), then "If we ask… we must also ask…" (×6).

### 1d. The antithesis alternation

*Shape:* two labeled abstractions traded line by line.

> "Holy certainty produces humility. Counterfeit certainty produces arrogance." (Ch. 7)

Chapter 7 runs this pairing **six consecutive times** in a single centered block. Chapter 13 does it with Performance vs. Testimony (four pairs). The current master's Chapter 1 does it with unrenewed vs. renewed mind (three pairs, set off with ◆ dividers). One of these per book is an aria; three is a template.

### 1e. The rhetorical-question string

*Shape:* three-plus unanswered questions in a row. Ch. 9: three consecutive "What if the thing you call X is actually Y?" questions. Ch. 12: five consecutive "Why does…?" bullets. The Introduction opens with a seven-question bullet list. Question-mark density spikes in Ch. 1 (60), Ch. 5 (60), and Ch. 8 (68) — versus ~15–25 in restrained chapters, so some chapters interrogate the reader four times as often as others, and strings of questions stop functioning as questions; they become weather.

### 1f. The escalation ladder ("Then… Then… Then…")

*Shape:* a slippery-slope sequence of quoted inner speech. Current master Ch. 1: "I'm just not sure God really said that" → not sure Scripture can be trusted → not sure anyone can know truth → not sure sin is sin → not sure I need God (five rungs). Ch. 9 (V2): "When planning becomes peace. When preparation becomes trust. When security becomes savior." This one earns its keep more often than the others — but it, too, appears in some form in at least five chapters.

### 1g. The centered one-line aphorism box

V2's most visually obvious tic: a boxed, centered, bold-italic pronouncement between paragraphs. Counts per chapter: Ch. 1: 6 · Ch. 2: 23 · Ch. 3: 24 · Ch. 4: **64** · Ch. 5: **65** · Ch. 6: **79** · Ch. 7: **78** · Ch. 8: 40 · Ch. 9: 67 · Ch. 10: 29 · Ch. 11: 28 · Ch. 12: 35 · Ch. 13: 63 · Ch. 14: 73 · Ch. 15: 54 — **~737 total**, plus ~311 "◆ ◆ ◆" section dividers. In Chapters 6–7 that's one aphorism box every **70–75 words**. When every third paragraph is italicized, centered, and bolded, emphasis has no unmarked ground to stand out from. The current master has largely (and rightly) dissolved these into prose.

### 1h. The formulaic section opener

"One of the greatest/most/oldest X is Y" opens a section **13 times** in V2 (neutral education, Ch. 1; science vs. faith, Ch. 2; the cosmological argument and the watch, Ch. 4; the silence of God, Ch. 5; neutrality, Ch. 8; control, Ch. 8; identity, Ch. 10; fear of man, Ch. 10; deliverance, Ch. 13; conviction vs. transformation, Ch. 15…). Its sibling is the landing sentence — "That changes everything," "That distinction is everything," "That is the moral trap" — which closes argumentative beats the way a preacher taps the pulpit.

### 1i. "Not because A. Not because B. But because C."

Chapter 14 opens with four "Not because…" fragments before the "But because" turn; Chapter 7 runs the same triple. A compressed cousin ("not X, but Y" inside one sentence) appears ~100 times across both documents — 19 times in the current master's Chapter 1 alone.

## 2. Structural Repetition Across Chapters: The Macro-Template

This is the deeper problem, and no amount of line editing touches it. **Chapters 4–15 of V2 — twelve consecutive chapters — share an identical closing sequence in identical order:**

| Slot | Fixed content, every chapter |
|---|---|
| 1 | Cold open: italic epigraph → ◆ ◆ ◆ → an abstract pronouncement ("There is a kind of certainty…", "Control is one of the most respectable idols…", "Righteousness has an image problem," "Beauty is a strange kind of evidence") |
| 2 | 15–22 argument sections, each ~200–350 words, each opened by an ALL-CAPS heading, each separated by ◆ ◆ ◆ |
| 3 | **WHY THIS MATTERS FOR THE SKEPTIC** |
| 4 | **WHY THIS MATTERS FOR THE BELIEVER** |
| 5 | **CLOSING CHARGE:** + imperative ("DO NOT BOW TO THE AGE," "PUT YOUR WEIGHT DOWN," "BECOME THE EVIDENCE," "FOLLOW BEAUTY HOME," "LET GOD BE GOD"…) |
| 6 | **REASONING REFLECTION** → Quote to Reflect On → What We Covered → Ponder/Write → Prayer |

Twelve chapters, six slots, zero variation. Beyond the slots, the chapters also repeat the same *argumentative* macro-move — call it **the borrowed-capital turn**: (1) name a cultural claim; (2) show the claimant secretly depends on the thing they deny; (3) conclude that only Christ funds what they've been spending. It powers Ch. 3 (relativism refutes itself), Ch. 4 (the universe can't explain itself), Ch. 5 (evil assumes a good standard), Ch. 6 (ought assumes a lawgiver), Ch. 8 (the atheist has faith), Ch. 11 (judging power requires a standard above power), and Ch. 12 (beauty exceeds survival value). It is a genuinely good move — it is *the* move of the book — but executed seven times with the same three beats, it reads as one chapter wearing seven costumes.

Two other macro-moves recur on schedule: the **accusation-of-the-age opening** (most chapters begin by indicting a cultural lie before any person appears on the page) and the **myth/lie inventory** (Ch. 1's lie of neutral education; Ch. 2's myths of blind faith — five numbered myths in the current master; Ch. 8's myth of neutrality; Ch. 9's false promise of security).

Notably, the *only* extended dramatized scene in either document is Pilate's chamber in Chapter 3 — and it's one of the best passages you've written. Its rarity is the structural diagnosis in miniature: the book has one narrative gear and twenty argument gears.

**"What We Covered" compounds the problem.** Each chapter already states its thesis in the opening box, restates it in aphorism boxes every ~100 words, restates it for the skeptic, restates it for the believer, restates it in the closing charge — and then summarizes it again in prose. A typical V2 chapter tells the reader its one idea eight to ten times.

## 4. List-itis

Bullet-line counts per V2 chapter tell the story at a glance: Chapters 3–7 contain **zero** bullet lines, then Part 4–5 collapse into outline mode — Ch. 10: 76 · Ch. 11: **220** · Ch. 12: 120 · Ch. 13: 136 · Ch. 14: **244** · Ch. 15: 164. Roughly **1,050 bulleted lines** book-wide. Much of Parts 4–5 is not yet prose; it is a sermon outline wearing a book's clothing. Chapter 11's believer section is nine consecutive "Do not…" commands; Chapter 14 renders even *Jesus' actions* as bullets ("He steps into history… He speaks… He calls…" — eight rungs); Chapter 15's climactic charge is a fourteen-bullet ladder at the exact moment the book most needs momentum.

Bullets flatten momentum because they refuse to subordinate: every item arrives with equal weight, so nothing builds toward anything. The current master has already learned this — its only surviving list is Chapter 1's nine-question discernment checklist, which is defensible as a genuine *tool* the reader might return to. That's the standard to keep: **a list must be a tool, not a rhythm.** Everything else becomes sentences, where "and," "but," and "until" can do the ranking work bullets can't.

The related tic is the **question string** (Intro's seven-bullet opening; Ch. 12's five "Why does…?" lines). One real question, held long enough to hurt, outworks five that scroll past.

## 5. Density and Pacing: No Room to Breathe — from Two Directions

The two documents fail in *opposite* directions, which is useful, because it means the target register lies between drafts you've already written.

**V2 suffocates by fragmentation.** Across Chapters 4–7, mean sentence length is **12.1 words, and 45% of sentences are seven words or fewer**. With an aphorism box every 70–110 words and a ◆ ◆ ◆ every ~300, the reader is never allowed to be *inside* an argument — only to be handed its conclusions, one epigram at a time. All-climax is no-climax: when every line is a mic-drop, the mic never lands. There is also almost no narrative oxygen — no people, no rooms, no time passing — so the abstractions have nothing to push against.

**The current master suffocates by accumulation.** Mean sentence length jumps to **23.3 words, with 19% of sentences running 35+ words**. Chapter 1's opening sentence stacks four appositive clauses ("a thought that… a wound that… a slogan that… a desire that…") before its first period; several paragraphs chain five or six clauses with comma splices ("…they can feed into the brokenness… or they can choose… The man who feeds his brokenness may not be different at first but over time…"). The reader gets no full stops to breathe *at*, which produces the same claustrophobia as V2's fragments by the opposite mechanism.

What both drafts lack is the **middle register**: the 15–25 word working sentence that carries an argument forward without either gasping or drowning — and the *paragraph-level* variation of one scene, one image, or one held silence between argumentative pushes. (This is precisely the "never fall into the parallel-stack drone" rule your `reasoning-faith-voice` spec already names; the audit confirms the spec is aimed at the right target.)

## 6. The Fix List: Ten Highest-Leverage De-Repetition Moves

Ranked by leverage — structural moves first, because sentence-level fixes cannot save a repeated skeleton.

1. **Break the twelve-chapter closing template (V2 Ch. 4–15).** Keep the Reasoning Reflection block as the book's *one* fixed ritual — a ritual is only a ritual if it's the only one. Dissolve "Why This Matters for the Skeptic/Believer" into the body: address the skeptic at the moment the argument naturally turns toward them, not in a scheduled appendix. Let closing charges vary in *form* — end one chapter on a story, one on a single line, one mid-image — instead of twelve titled imperatives.

2. **Merge the moral material of Chapters 5 and 6.** Ch. 6 gets the whole meta-ethical argument (ought → lawgiver); Ch. 5 becomes the pastoral chapter it wants to be (suffering, Job, silence, the cross) and *promises* the trap rather than springing it early. This deletes the book's most flagrant idea-repeat: two consecutive chapters opening with the same Not-ladder to the same conclusion.

3. **Give "everyone trusts something" one home.** Full treatment in Ch. 2; one-sentence callbacks elsewhere ("we settled in chapter two that no one comes to this question empty-handed"). Rewrite Ch. 8 to do only what it uniquely does — belief as allegiance, belief under pressure, Abraham — and cut its three re-argument sections.

4. **Ration the Not-ladder to roughly three appearances in the whole book.** Keep Ch. 4's "Not matter. Not chaos. Not nothing. Not chance. God." — it's the best one and it's load-bearing. Convert the rest to ordinary subordinated prose. The move's power is inversely proportional to its frequency; at three uses it will detonate, at forty it's wallpaper.

5. **De-box V2 (or carry the current master's de-boxing forward).** Budget: at most 3 display aphorisms per chapter, down from 29–79. Promote only lines that survive being read *aloud, twice*; run the rest into paragraphs. Same for ◆ ◆ ◆: one or two per chapter at genuine hinges, not twenty.

6. **Convert Parts 4–5 from outline to prose.** Chapters 10–15's ~960 bullet lines are drafting scaffolds, not finished pages. Rule going forward: one list per chapter, and only when the list is a tool (Ch. 1's discernment checklist passes; Ch. 14's bulleted Jesus does not).

7. **Vary the chapter openings — put people in them.** Of sixteen openings, roughly thirteen begin with an abstract pronouncement about the age. Open at least half the chapters inside a scene: Pilate's chamber (Ch. 3) proves you can do this at a high level — Thomas (Ch. 7), the rich young ruler (Ch. 9), and the Ch. 5 sufferer who prayed into silence are all sitting in your own chapters waiting to be moved to the front door.

8. **Fix cadence from both directions.** In V2 chapters: fuse staccato runs — take three consecutive sub-seven-word sentences and subordinate two of them. In the current master: split every 35+-word clause-chain at its strongest joint and repair the comma splices. Target a mixed profile where a short sentence is an *event* again.

9. **Retire the default connective tissue.** "One of the greatest/most X is Y" (13 section openings), the "That is…/That changes everything" landing tap, and the "X is not Y. It is Z." definition formula (every ~850 words in the current master). None is wrong once; all are load-bearing walls of the drone. For each occurrence, either cut the frame and start with the claim itself, or find the version only you would write.

10. **Name the borrowed-capital turn once, then trust it.** In Chapter 1 (or the Introduction), tell the reader plainly: *this book's method is to ask what each objection must borrow in order to object.* Then each later chapter can execute a *variation* — Ch. 12 through wonder and testimony rather than gotcha, Ch. 11 through history, Ch. 6 through conscience — instead of re-performing the same three beats incognito seven times. A named move repeated is a theme; an unnamed move repeated is a rut.

## Appendix: Notes on the Current Master (Ch. 1–3 Rewrite)

The rewrite is a real improvement — boxes dissolved, bullets nearly gone, arguments allowed to run at paragraph length, and new material (the mouth/heart agreement thread in Ch. 2) that gives the book a pastoral center V2's Ch. 2 lacked. Three watch-items as the rewrite proceeds into Chapters 4–6:

- **Word-study frequency.** Six Greek/Hebrew studies in three chapters (*natsar*, *hupostasis*, *elegchos*, *dipsuchos*, *kathorao*, *aletheia*), all on the same frame: "The Greek word translated X is Y, which literally means Z…" At two per chapter, the frame itself becomes a skeleton. Suggest one per chapter, and vary the entrance (sometimes give the meaning before naming the Greek).
- **Run-on and splice repair** (see fix #8) — the rewrite traded V2's fragments for chains; the middle register is still the open ground.
- **The dictionary-definition sidebar** (Ch. 1's "access | ˈak-ˌses" block) is a fresh device — good. Keep it *rare* so it stays fresh.

**Erratum (V2):** in Chapter 5, a stray "CHAPTER 6" heading is pasted mid-sentence — "…nor to hasten the process of CHAPTER 6 / mourning…" — a copy artifact worth fixing before any further work from that file.
