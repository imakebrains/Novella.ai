# The Reasoning Faith Voice — Personality Integration Plan

**Purpose:** define, positively, what "Drew's personality on the page" means for
*Reasoning Faith*, and give the named, repeatable moves that put it there —
inside the book's standing rules (no em dashes, no contractions in body prose,
KJV verified, one heart addressed, Chesterton/Pascal/Tozer fusion at the line
level).

**Sources:** the book's own strongest material (the Dedication and Chapters
1–3 of the current master), the two comparison-book analyses in `analysis/`,
and the genre/craft research in `research/`. This plan deliberately does NOT
draw on the author's fiction or private writing: the author has ruled that the
fiction voice stays out of this book, and private material stays private.

---

## 1. The governing decision

From the author interview (see `notes/interview-2026-08-17.md`):

> *Reasoning Faith* stays **educational, informative, and self-convicting** —
> the reader analyzes themselves through the Word of God. No imported wit.
> Personality enters through **personal vignettes and veiled true stories**,
> **emotional precision**, and **directness**, written first to the author's
> **younger self** at his point of deepest doubt.

This is not a limitation; it is a clarification. The *Occupy the Algorithm*
analysis proved the point empirically: strip every joke out of that manuscript
and its persona survives intact — carried by confession, concrete specificity,
voiced objections, conviction derived from the reader's own premises, and the
open question left standing. **Humor is an accent. Presence is the voice.**
*Reasoning Faith* needs presence.

## 2. What "personality" means in this register

Personality in a teaching book is not tone. It is **a person remaining on the
page** — someone with a history, a wound, a stake in the argument, and the
honesty to be examined by the same Scripture he is holding up to the reader.
Wherever the person leaves the page, the prose could belong to any manual;
wherever he stays, even dense doctrine feels like testimony.

The book already contains the proof of its own voice. Four exhibits:

1. **The Dedication.** Wound, shame, and grace, confessed in rhythm. This is
   the register the chapters are starving for — the check the rest of the
   book must cash in installments.
2. **The Pilate scene (Ch. 3).** A dramatized room, a career, a crowd, a
   prisoner who does not act like the one on trial — then the turn that makes
   the scene a mirror. Proof the author can write scenes, not just points.
3. **The discovered-lie passage (Ch. 3).** "Remember what happened in your
   chest" — emotional perception precise enough that the reader feels known.
4. **"Conflict wearing the shape of a prayer request" (Ch. 2).** The
   compressed, quotable line that Pascal's instinct produces when the thought
   and the feeling arrive together.

The fingerprint, read off those exhibits: plain-elevated diction with KJV
gravity; imagery of gates, fires, rooms, and roads; a pastoral ache underneath
the argument; aphorisms earned by pressure; and a habit of seeing the reader's
interior with uncomfortable accuracy. What is missing is not skill. It is
**self-disclosure** — the author being seen while he sees.

## 3. The twelve voice moves

Each move is named so it can be requested, drafted to, and audited. Example
sketches are written fresh, in register, as *shapes* — the true details are
the author's to supply.

### 1. Confession before charge
Never ask the reader for an honesty not already performed on the page. Before
any passage that examines the reader (who has access to your mind, is your
faith divided, what comfort was never true), the author goes first — one
concrete admission, then the question.
*Shape:* "There was a year I let one voice past the gate simply because it
kept arriving. I did not weigh it. I welcomed it. So when I ask who has been
discipling your mind, understand that I am asking as a man who once left the
gate unmanned."

### 2. The witness-protection reversal
The manuscript already contains real memories disguised as "you" and "a
person" (the audit calls this circling a memory without telling it). Where
the material can bear it, take off the disguise: convert the second-person
hypothetical to first-person testimony, with thirty percent more specific
detail. Veiling of other people is permitted and often right; veiling of the
author defeats the purpose.

### 3. The lived cold open
Open chapters inside a moment, not under a thesis. A particular night, a
particular room, something already in motion. The doctrine arrives as the
resolution of that moment. (The Pilate scene proves the skill; the move is to
open with the author's own rooms, not only Scripture's.)

### 4. Scene first, sentence second
The aphorism is placed at the structural boundary, after the experience that
earns it. Never lead with the quotable line; let it land as the verdict of a
staged moment. One underline-worthy line per section remains the standard.

### 5. Derived conviction
Conviction lands as invitation when it is deduced from the reader's own
confessed premise rather than pronounced from the platform.
*Shape:* "You believe your mind is your own. Good. A man who owns a thing
guards it. So the question is not whether you are free but whether the gate
of a free man should stand open all night."

### 6. The single held question
One question, aimed and left standing, outworks a battery of five. Question
strings read as a security checkpoint; a single question after a confession
reads as a hand on the shoulder. Ration the interrogatives; keep the one that
hurts.

### 7. The unshockable confessor stance
The reader must feel that nothing they bring — doubt, anger at God, the
prayer that embarrassed them, the wound they blame Him for — could make the
author think less of them. This is Yancey's documented power and the
Dedication's implicit promise. In practice: name the shameful versions of the
struggle explicitly, without flinching and without scolding, before
addressing them.

### 8. Steelman, then turn
Already law in the voice skill; the comparison analyses add the structural
version: let the skeptic's position be stated at its most sympathetic — the
form its holder would applaud — and where possible let the *resistant* voice
be the one that walks the reader across (the hostile-witness principle).
The author's own former self is the book's best hostile witness.

### 9. The totemic object
Attach each abstract truth to one ordinary object with a history, introduced
early and returned to later, so the doctrine has a body the reader can
picture. Grace, the divided heart, the guarded gate — each deserves its own
jar of peanut butter (see the *First Love Effect* analysis for how objects
carry memory and payoff). Two or three objects per book, not per chapter;
they gain meaning on every return.

### 10. Falsifiable specificity
Authority and intimacy both come from auditable detail: the year, the month
of nights, the number of times, the exact words of the thought. Round,
unverifiable generality ("many people," "we often") is the sound of the
lectern. Specificity is the sound of a witness under oath.

### 11. The middle-register working sentence
The book's two drafts fail in opposite directions: V2 gasps in fragments,
the current master drowns in clause-chains. The voice lives in the 15–25
word working sentence, varied hard, so that a short sentence is an event
again and a long one is a crescendo rather than a pileup.

### 12. Prayer as final callback
The chapter's closing prayer is written last and written specific: it echoes
this chapter's images and concedes this chapter's named doubts, so the
Reasoning Reflection descends from the chapter like a diminuendo instead of
switching genres. A prayer that could be swapped between chapters is filler.

## 4. Intimacy inside the formal register

The standing rules (no contractions, no em dashes, KJV) do not prevent
closeness; wrongly handled they merely make distance easier. The mechanics
that produce closeness without loosening the register:

- **Singular, situational address.** "You" is one person in one situation
  ("the night you stopped praying mid-sentence"), never a demographic.
- **Concession as warmth.** "Of course you are clever. Cleverness was never
  the point." Granting the reader their strongest card is the formal
  register's version of a smile.
- **Short plain words at the peaks.** Tozer's instinct: the weightiest
  moments use the smallest words. Formality that never simplifies reads as
  armor; formality that breaks into plainness at the point of feeling reads
  as sincerity.
- **The confession carries the warmth.** A voice that says "I was the man at
  the door" can keep every formal rule and still be embraced. Disclosure does
  the work contractions would have done.
- **Scripture as shared hearth, not exhibit.** Vary the entrance to word
  studies (sometimes meaning before Greek; one study per chapter), and let
  the verse arrive where the chapter aches for it rather than on schedule.

## 5. What stays out

- All of it: the banter, the running gags, the self-deprecating comedy, the
  pop-culture riffs. That voice belongs to the fiction and stays there.
- Em dashes, contractions in body prose, reader taxonomies, self-reference
  ("this chapter"), stacked shorts, repeated examples — per the master style.
- Manufactured rawness. The disclosure floor is specificity of *feeling and
  cost*; identifying detail is optional and governed by the veiling rules in
  `notes/story-inventory.md`. Scars, not wounds: nothing goes on the page
  that the author has not survived far enough to narrate with perspective.

## 6. The presence check (added to the section self-check)

Run alongside the reasoning-faith-voice skill's nine-point check, per section:

1. **Where am I on this page?** If the author appears nowhere in the chapter,
   the chapter is not finished.
2. **Did I go first?** Find the chapter's point of highest demand on the
   reader; confirm the author's own payment appears before it.
3. **Does my "I" risk anything?** A disclosure with a library card's stakes
   teaches the reader that all disclosures will be safe ones. One real cost
   per chapter.
4. **Is the reader one person?** Read the direct address aloud; if it could
   be a survey category, rewrite it to the younger self in the worst month.
5. **Would he have kept reading?** The final test of every chapter: not
   "is this true" but "would the man the Dedication describes have put this
   book down."
