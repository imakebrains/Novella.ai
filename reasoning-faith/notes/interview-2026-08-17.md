# Author interview — 2026-08-17

Four questions asked at the start of the personality/story-driving project.
These answers govern everything in `voice/` and `analysis/`.

## 1. Which comparison books?

**Both**: *Occupy the Algorithm* by Evan DiDio (Christian nonfiction — church
media/evangelism; working title "YouTube for Churches") and *The First Love
Effect*. Evan's book is the closer comp (nonfiction teaching that stays
personality- and story-driven); *The First Love Effect* supplies the pure
story-attachment mechanics.

## 2. How much of Drew's own story goes on the page?

**A mix of personal vignettes and veiled/composite stories.** Short true
scenes and confessions, one or two per chapter, grounding each argument in a
lived moment. Where a real situation is identifiable or involves another
person, details are disguised or composited. No continuous memoir spine, and
no obligation to expose anything raw on the page.

## 3. Who is the "one heart" the book speaks to?

All of the candidates resonate (a skeptic he loves, a drifting believer, an
online-formed searcher) — **but the primary reader is Drew's younger self**:
the book he needed at the point of his own deepest doubt and wound. Practical
consequence: every chapter should pass the test "would this have reached me
then, or would I have put it down?" That reader already supplies the other
three audiences, because he was, at different moments, all of them.

## 4. Voice register

**The fiction voice stays out. Completely.** No imported wit, banter, or
self-deprecation from the novel writing. *Reasoning Faith* remains
educational, informative, and **self-convicting** — the goal is that the
reader analyzes themselves through the Word of God. Personality enters
through:

- lived scenes and honest confession (per answer 2),
- emotional precision (naming what the reader actually feels),
- directness of address,
- the established Chesterton/Pascal/Tozer fused voice at the line level.

Not through humor.

## Standing constraints (from the book's style system)

- KJV Scripture, verified; Greek/Hebrew word studies italicized.
- No em dashes; no contractions in body prose; no reader categories;
  speaks to one heart.
- Reasoning Reflection (verse / ponder / prayer) closes chapters.
- Parallel cadence rationed: one deliberate hammer per section.
